# Fan Community & Profiles — Design

**Date:** 2026-08-16
**Status:** Awaiting review
**Repo:** `~/Desktop/MorphicsBrain/website` (morphicsmusic.com)

---

## 1. Why

The site currently gives a fan exactly one place of their own: `/library`, a
download list. Nothing there makes a fan feel like they have a standing at
morphicsmusic.com, and nothing gives them a reason to come back between
releases.

This project builds that standing. The goal is growth, and the mechanism is a
collectible identity: **fans earn avatars for things they do, other fans see
those avatars, and wanting one is what drives the next action.** A Perception
medallion on the leaderboard is both a status symbol and an advertisement for
the Perception album — the reward markets the thing that earns it.

## 2. Scope

This is the first of four sub-projects. It builds the **substrate** — profiles,
identity, the avatar system, and the community surface — with deliberate
sockets for the three that follow.

| Sub-project | This spec |
|---|---|
| Profile, avatars, directory, leaderboard | **In scope** |
| Free song of the week | Out — avatars defined, granted once it exists |
| Loyalty ranks & perks | Out — rank is a placeholder here |
| Shows & attendance | Out — avatars defined, granted once it exists |

Avatars for streaks, shows and gate completions are **defined and displayed as
locked** in v1. They cannot be earned until their source system ships. This is
deliberate: a locked avatar with a visible hint is a teaser, and teasers are
what make people seek things out.

## 3. Decisions taken

| Decision | Choice |
|---|---|
| Visibility | Semi-public — visible only to signed-in fans. Never to the open web. |
| Visible surface | Display name, avatar, *fan since*, collection. Plus rank and shows as sockets — both render in v1, but stay placeholders until their own sub-projects ship. |
| Hidden always | Email, prices paid, purchase dates, order history |
| Avatar source | Curated — release artwork, plus a small special class |
| Avatar role | **Unlockable rewards**, not decoration |
| Unlock sources | Release ownership, tenure, free-song streaks, shows, gate completions |
| Discovery | Full directory **and** leaderboard |
| Storage | D1 projection; KV remains source of truth for purchases |
| Home | A new **Community** tab at `/community` |

## 4. Architecture

### 4.1 Storage: D1 projection, KV untouched

The leaderboard forces this. Ranking across all fans means ordering and
scanning, and KV can only fetch `customer:<email>` by exact key — a leaderboard
in KV would mean listing every key and fetching every record on every page load.
Avatar rarity ("6% of fans have this") is the same problem.

So profile data lives in **D1**, alongside the gate tables. Purchases, download
grants, sessions and identity stay in **KV**, exactly as they are. Nothing in
`checkout.ts`, `stripe-webhook.ts`, `download.ts` or `library.ts` changes.

The D1 tables split into two kinds of field, and the distinction is what makes
this safe:

- **Derived** (`fan_since`, collection count, unlock ledger) — computed from KV
  and the catalogue. A rebuild script can regenerate all of it from scratch.
- **Fan-owned** (display name, handle, equipped avatar) — exists only in D1,
  never derived, never overwritten by a rebuild.

Drift between the two stores is therefore always a recoverable inconvenience,
never data loss. That property is the whole reason this approach beats moving
customer records into D1 wholesale — which would mean migrating live buyer data
and rewriting the fulfilment path.

### 4.2 The avatar catalogue

Two kinds of avatar, one table.

**Release avatars** — one per release, art derived from
`public/images/albums/<slug>.jpg`, which already exists for every release. The
catalogue row is generated by a sync script that reads `music-catalog.json`, so
**every future release ships with its avatar automatically** and no per-avatar
design work is ever needed. Unlock rule: own that release.

Rendered as a **circular medallion with a consistent frame**, not a raw square
cover. A leaderboard of bare album covers reads as a discography rather than as
people; the crop and frame keep it recognisably Perception while still reading
as somebody's face.

**Special avatars** — a small hand-authored class for things ownership cannot
express: tenure milestones, free-song streaks, specific shows, gate campaigns.
These may be time-limited (`available_from` / `available_until`), which makes
them unrepeatable and therefore genuinely scarce.

**Rarity is emergent, not assigned.** An early or obscure release is worn by few
people and is thereby rare, with no tiering work. Surfaced as a live percentage.

### 4.3 The unlock engine

A pure function, testable without any I/O:

```
evaluateUnlocks(context, catalogue) -> avatarId[]
```

where `context` is `{ ownedSlugs, fanSince, now, streakWeeks, showsAttended,
gatesCompleted }`. It returns every avatar the fan qualifies for; the caller
inserts them with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, making the whole operation
idempotent.

Idempotency buys something valuable: running it for the first time **backfills
every existing customer**. Your current buyers sign in and find a profile that
already has their collection on it, rather than an empty shelf that punishes
them for having bought things before this existed.

It runs on sign-in and on profile load. Both are already authenticated paths.

### 4.4 Routes

All under `/community`, all auth-gated, all `noindex`.

| Route | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `/community` | Directory + leaderboard |
| `/community/me` | Your own profile — editable |
| `/community/u/<handle>` | Another fan's profile |
| `/library` | **Unchanged.** Remains the download surface. |

`u/` segments the fan namespace so later additions (`/community/shows`) can't
collide with a handle.

Signed-out visitors to any `/community` route get a sign-in prompt explaining
what's behind it — a conversion surface, not a dead end.

## 5. Data model

D1 migration `0002_fan_profiles.sql`, reversible, purely additive.

**`fan_profiles`** — one row per fan.
`id`, `email` (unique, the link to `customer:<email>`), `handle` (unique,
URL-safe), `display_name`, `equipped_avatar_id`, `fan_since`, `rank_points`
(placeholder, default 0), `collection_count`, `created_at`, `updated_at`,
`last_seen_at`.

**`avatar_catalogue`** — one row per avatar.
`id`, `kind` (`release` | `special`), `release_slug` (nullable), `name`,
`art_path`, `unlock_rule` (JSON), `hint` (the line shown while locked),
`available_from`, `available_until`, `sort_order`.

**`fan_avatar_unlocks`** — the ledger.
`id`, `fan_id`, `avatar_id`, `unlocked_at`, `source`, `source_ref`,
`UNIQUE(fan_id, avatar_id)`.

Indexes: `fan_profiles.handle`, `fan_profiles.email`, `fan_avatar_unlocks
(avatar_id)` for rarity counts, `fan_avatar_unlocks (fan_id)` for a profile's
shelf.

## 6. Privacy

- Email is never exposed in any API response or page. The **handle** is the
  only fan-facing identifier.
- No prices, purchase dates, or order history is exposed anywhere, to anyone.
- Collection is shown as **artwork**, not as a purchase list. Because giveaways
  and free-song claims land in the same shelf as purchases, an onlooker cannot
  tell what was bought and what was claimed. Privacy through ambiguity, which
  works precisely because the giveaway volume is high.
- Every `/community` route requires a valid session and sets `noindex`.
- Display names run through a blocklist filter on save.

## 7. Ranking in v1

Rank is a **placeholder**, not the loyalty system. The leaderboard orders by
avatars unlocked, then by tenure. `rank_points` exists as a column so the
loyalty sub-project has somewhere to write, and the profile has a slot to show
a tier badge.

Noted for that sub-project: **collection size is a poor rank input** while
giveaways are frequent, because it rewards whoever claims the most free items
rather than whoever is most invested. Avatars tied to specific behaviours do not
have this problem, which is why they carry the weight in v1.

## 8. Out of scope

Uploaded avatars (moderation liability, explicitly rejected). Public profiles
or SEO exposure. Rank tiers and perks. Show attendance capture. Free-song
rotation. Fan-to-fan messaging or comments — no moderation capacity exists, and
nothing in the goal requires it.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| KV↔D1 drift | Derived fields regenerable by a rebuild script; fan-owned fields never derived |
| Handle squatting / abuse | Blocklist on save; handles are unique and changeable by admin |
| Backfill cost on first sign-in | Bounded by catalogue size (~25 rows); runs once, then no-ops |
| Five nav items crowd mobile | Shorten the label or rework the breakpoint; `BottomNav.astro` also needs a look |
| Empty leaderboard at launch | Backfill means existing buyers appear immediately rather than day-one being blank |

## 10. Open questions

1. Handle chosen by the fan, or derived from display name with a numeric
   suffix on collision?
2. Should the equipped avatar be freely switchable among unlocked ones, or
   does the rarest auto-equip?
3. Does the directory paginate, or is the fanbase small enough to list whole?

None block starting implementation; all three are cheap to change later.
